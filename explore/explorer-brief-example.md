# A worked explore brief

One brief you could actually send, plus notes on why each part is there.

**Adapt the shape, not the specifics.** The topic, the folders, and the search terms below are invented for the example. Four parts you'll write every time: *what it's for*, *where to look*, *don't summarize*, *the return skeleton*. The rest depends on the job.

The example: you're gathering material for a note on **how you decide what to work on**, something you've thought about in a hundred places and never collected.

---

## The brief

```text
EXPLORE BRIEF — "how I decide what to work on"

Read this fully before you search.

YOUR TERRITORY
  Search only:  projects/          (~200 files)
  Skip:         library/, sessions/ (other agents have those)
  Start at the repository root.

  If you find something valuable outside your territory, mention it in
  one line at the end. Don't chase it.

THE CONCEPT
  How I actually choose what gets my attention: the real mechanism, not
  the tidy version. What makes me drop something, what makes me pick it
  back up, and what I say when I'm avoiding a decision.

WHAT THIS IS FOR
  A thinking-map for me. NOT a productivity system, not advice, not
  something I'm publishing. Don't flag anything as "ready to share."

WHAT I ALREADY HAVE — extend it, don't return it
  I have the obvious material: anywhere I've explicitly written
  "priorities" or "what matters most." Don't hand those back. I want the
  times I made the decision without ever calling it one.

FIND ESPECIALLY
  - Moments I abandoned something: the reasoning in the moment, not the
    tidy summary written afterward
  - Any version of "I keep coming back to this"
  - Decisions made against the obvious choice, and what I said at the time
  - Places where my stated reason and my actual behavior don't match

  I might be wrong that these patterns are there. If the material doesn't
  support one, say so. Telling me my frame is off is worth more to me
  than confirming it.

SEARCH TERMS
  Start here:   "keep coming back", "torn between", "gave up on",
                "instead of", "pulling at me"
  Then:         decide, decision, priority, dropped, parked, shelved
  Noisy — only follow these when something else is already interesting:
                later, should I, not now, what matters

  Beyond keywords: decisions made in passing; what I chose *over* what;
  the same tension recurring in different words; anywhere I argue with
  myself.

SIZE AND STOPPING
  Aim for 15–40 passages. If you find more, keep the ones where I'm
  arguing with myself.

  If your territory turns out to be thin, return less and say so. A short
  honest return is worth more to me than a padded one. Don't promote
  weak matches to fill space.

TEXTURE RULES
  For every match: file path, line numbers, and 5–10 lines of surrounding
  context. Verbatim. Do NOT summarize or paraphrase — I want what was
  actually said, messy phrasing included. If I swore, keep the swearing.

  If the passage that matters runs longer than 10 lines, keep it whole and
  note that you did.

MAP BEFORE YOU SEARCH
  List your territory's subfolders and read any orientation file in each
  before searching deeply. Build the map first. Architecture is
  information.

WHAT TO RETURN
  Organized raw findings grouped by theme. Do NOT return a finished
  document, a summary table, or recommendations. Synthesis is my job once
  I've seen every agent's return. Your job is organized raw material.

  ## projects/ — findings

  ### [theme you noticed, in your words]
  - path/to/file (L120-129):
    > verbatim quote, 5-10 lines
    [one line: why this one matters]

  ### 🎈 WHAT PULLED AT ME
  [What did you find that my categories had no box for?]

  ### 📋 WHAT I'D CHANGE ABOUT THIS BRIEF
  [What did you need and not get? What did I tell you that turned out
   wrong? What would the next agent sent here want to know?]
```

## Why each part is there

**Your territory** — an agent with no scope searches everything, and if you've sent several, they all cover the same ground. Give a real path and a rough size; a folder name means nothing until it knows whether that's 40 files or 4,000. The overlap line matters because otherwise it silently picks a policy (drop it or chase it) and doesn't tell you which. *Scope applies even when you send one agent.*

**What this is for** — agents shape output toward an assumed downstream use, invisibly. One that thinks you're publishing will quietly filter for the presentable and drop the embarrassing, which here is the entire point. **Name the negative**; a positive-only framing doesn't stop it.

**What I already have** — the most valuable paragraph and the easiest to skip. Without it, an agent establishes the obvious first, because that's what covering the ground looks like from the inside. You pay a whole run to be handed back what you already knew.

**Find especially** — give *shapes*, not keywords. "Any version of 'I keep coming back to this'" can be pattern-matched across phrasings; a keyword can't. And the closing caveat is load-bearing: **a confident brief gets its frame confirmed.** Hand over a specific pattern and it will be found, including where it isn't there. Your framing sets the threshold for what counts as a match, and the evidence that contradicts you never clears that bar, so it never enters the return. **You cannot detect this from what comes back; it looks like success.** One sentence inviting the disconfirmation is the only defense.

**Search terms** — agents are bad at guessing your vocabulary and good at grep, so give them your actual words. But rank them and mark the noisy ones: a flat list of sixteen creates an implicit obligation to show hits across all sixteen, which drags the return toward the worst terms. Ranking turns a word list into a search strategy.

**Size and stopping** — the omission that costs the most. With no budget, an agent either stops early because the return looks substantial, or keeps going and then **compresses to fit**, silently undoing the "don't summarize" rule three lines below. And explicit permission to come back short is rare and worth a lot: returning almost nothing reads as failing, so without it, marginal matches get promoted to fill the shape.

**Texture rules** — agents compress by default, and compression destroys exactly what a texture-gathering run is for. Say it explicitly, every time. The unpolished phrasing is usually where the real thinking is.

**What to return** — put the reflection fields **in the output skeleton, not in the prose**. An instruction to "reflect on the brief" buried in a paragraph competes against ten other instructions and loses; a labeled empty field in the required shape is a hole, and holes get filled. The second field is the only channel through which a bad brief can tell you it was bad.
